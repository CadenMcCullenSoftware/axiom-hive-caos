## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

This document is the per-technique reference for the adversary-AI technique taxonomy. It exists so that three different readers can work from the same description of the same technique without three different vocabularies:

| Reader | What this document supplies |
| --- | --- |
| Analyst | A description of what the technique is, what it looks like when it happens, and what it tends to support in terms of attribution |
| Detection engineer | The observable artefacts and the candidate detection objectives — the predicates a detector could test |
| Operator and countermeasure designer | The candidate countermeasure bindings, and the expected detection latency that shapes how quickly a response can be mounted |

### 1.2 Relationship to the taxonomy document

`operational/adversary-ai-taxonomy-and-threat-model.md` owns the identity of the taxonomy: the identifier, the name, the definition, the observable artefact, the category assignment, and the scope boundaries. This document is the deeper per-technique reference that sits beneath it. The division is deliberate, and it is the division the coordinator's ruling of 2026-10-20 settled:

| Concern | Owner | This document |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technique identifiers, names, and definitions | Taxonomy document §5 | Reproduces the name and the definition, and builds its description on them |
| Observable artefact, as the taxonomy states it | Taxonomy document §5 | Reproduces it, and adds the library's further observable detail after it |
| Category structure and scope boundaries (AAT-1 to AAT-10) | Taxonomy document §4, §5 | Reproduces in summary |
| Further observable detail beyond the canonical artefact | This document | Authoritative within the set |
| Candidate detection objectives | This document | Authoritative within the set |
| Technique-level countermeasure bindings | This document | Authoritative within the set, superseding the indicative bindings at taxonomy document §5 |
| Countermeasure properties — class, collateral class, reversibility, autonomy class, preconditions, failure modes | Countermeasure catalogue | — |
| Adversary archetypes, capability profiles, threat modelling procedure | Taxonomy document §6 to §8 | — |
| External mapping verification status | This document | Authoritative; the register is at §18 |
| Methodological and evidence-handling requirements | Taxonomy document §4.3, §4.4 | — |

**Precedence.** Where this document and the taxonomy document differ on the identity or the behaviour of a technique, the taxonomy document prevails and the difference is a defect against this document. Where this document and the countermeasure catalogue differ on a countermeasure's class, collateral class, reversibility, autonomy class, preconditions, or failure modes, the catalogue prevails. A binding recorded here is a claim that a countermeasure is a candidate response to a technique; it is not a claim that the binding has been tested against the technique, and it does not imply admissibility under any rules-of-engagement profile.

### 1.3 What this document is not

- **Not an authorisation.** No technique described here authorises an action. Authorisation is established by the engagement authorisation and the Operation Card, and enforced by the policy engine. See `constitutional/engagement-authorisation-and-rules-of-engagement.md`.
- **Not a detection rule set.** The detection objectives in §6 to §15 are candidate predicates for a detection engineer to evaluate, not deployed rules. Detection engineering content, including the DET- requirements, sits with the taxonomy document and the platform architecture document.
- **Not a threat model.** Threat modelling, archetypes, and capability assessment sit with the taxonomy document.
- **Not a claim of attacker capability.** The techniques are described as behaviour patterns that have been reported or reasoned about in general practice. Nothing here states that a particular party has performed them, and nothing here states that a given technique is easy or hard to perform.
- **Not an external mapping.** The external mapping register in §18 is deliberately empty. The internal AAT identifier is canonical for this system.

### 1.4 The technique list

The list is the seventy-seven techniques at the taxonomy document §5, reproduced here in the same identifiers, the same order, and the same names. A technique identifier denotes the same behaviour in both documents. Where version 1.0 of this library used an identifier for a different behaviour, the entry has been re-keyed, and the record of what changed is at §20.3.

## 2. Verification Status Convention, and the Status of This Document's Content

### 2.1 Status set

| Status | Meaning in this document |
| --- | --- |
| **Verified (primary)** | Confirmed against the primary source cited for the claim. No technique row in this document carries this status; the register in §18 is where it would be recorded. |
| **Verified (secondary)** | Confirmed through a reputable secondary source that cites the primary source. No technique row in this document carries this status. |
| **Requires confirmation** | The underlying material exists and is real, but the specific claim was not located within it. Used in this document for the external mapping register entries and for the cross-document items in §21.2. |
| **Unverified** | Asserted, but not matched to a source. Used in this document for all descriptive technique content. |
| **Corrected** | A verdict or wording that has been replaced by a later, better-evidenced position. Recorded in this set for prose verdicts rather than for technique rows. |

### 2.2 The status applied to every technique

Every technique row in §6 to §15 carries the same status string:

> **Unverified — derived from general practice; no primary citation recorded**

**This is deliberate, and the document makes no claim of primary-source verification.** The descriptions, artefacts, detection objectives, and bindings in this document are authored from general operational and security practice. No primary publication has been cited for any of them, and no independent reviewer has confirmed them against a recorded source. The document therefore asserts them as a working reference, and nothing more.

The status attaches to the *descriptive content*. It does not attach to the identifiers, the technique names, the definitions, or the canonical observable artefacts, which are canonical by construction because they are owned by the taxonomy document and are reproduced here without alteration.

### 2.3 What a reader may do with an unverified technique row

| Permitted | Not permitted |
| --- | --- |
| Use the description and artefacts as a starting point for a detection hypothesis | Treat the artefact list as an exhaustive enumeration of what the technique leaves behind |
| Use the detection objectives to design and evaluate a candidate detector | Treat a candidate detection objective as a validated detection rule |
| Use the countermeasure bindings to build a candidate countermeasure set | Treat a binding as an endorsement of a countermeasure, or as a statement that the binding has been tested against the technique |
| Use the MTTD expectation to prioritise sensor investment | Treat the MTTD rating as a measured or guaranteed detection time |
| Use the attribution note to decide whether a countermeasure may proceed on class confidence alone | Treat the attribution note as an attribution finding about any particular observed activity |

### 2.4 Basis of the countermeasure bindings

The OCC identifiers and their names in this document are reproduced from the canonical countermeasure catalogue, `operational/countermeasure-catalogue.md`, and are exact. The catalogue remains authoritative for every countermeasure's class, collateral class, reversibility category, autonomy class, preconditions, failure modes, and attribution dependency. A binding recorded here is a claim that a countermeasure is a candidate response to a technique.

### 2.5 MTTD rating scale

The mean time to detection rating is **relative to the operator's telemetry estate**, assumed to collect endpoint, identity, network, and agent-plane telemetry. It is not an absolute time and it does not account for resourcing, analyst attention, or queueing.

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Low** | The behaviour leaves a direct record in a collection the operator already operates, visible at or near the time of the behaviour |
| **Medium** | The artefact is present but is not separable from legitimate activity without a baseline, a correlation, or a further collection |
| **High** | The artefact is visible only in aggregate, only after a delay, or only in a collection the operator may not have or may not routinely examine |

## 3. RFC 2119 Interpretation

The key words **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **REQUIRED**, **SHALL**, **SHALL NOT**, **SHOULD**, **SHOULD NOT**, **RECOMMENDED**, **MAY**, and **OPTIONAL** in this document are to be interpreted as described in RFC 2119, and only when they appear in capitals.

| Convention | Application in this document |
| --- | --- |
| **MUST** / **MUST NOT** | An absolute obligation on the reader or on the set. Used sparingly; principally for the fixed technique list and the verification-status discipline |
| **SHOULD** / **SHOULD NOT** | A strong recommendation. Departure requires a recorded reason |
| **MAY** | A genuinely optional course, with no obligation either way |
| Descriptive statements in the tables | Normative content is confined to the declared requirements, the fixed technique list, and the status conventions. Everything else is descriptive reference material and carries no obligation |

A binding, an artefact, or a detection objective recorded in this document is a **candidate**. Its presence creates no obligation to implement it and no obligation to select it.

## 4. Defined Terms

Terms are declared once here and used as defined thereafter.

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **AAT category** | One of AAT-1 to AAT-10; the behaviour-centred classification of adversary activity defined in the taxonomy document |
| **AAT technique** | One of the seventy-seven techniques AAT-1.1 to AAT-10.8, identified by the form `AAT-n.m` |
| **Canonical behaviour** | The behaviour a technique identifier denotes, as fixed by the taxonomy document §5. The identifier is the join key across the set |
| **Observable artefact** | A thing that appears in telemetry as a consequence of the technique, sufficient that a detector could test for it. An artefact is an observation, not an intent |
| **Detection objective** | A candidate observable predicate a detector could test. An objective is not a rule, a threshold, or a validated detection |
| **Countermeasure binding** | A candidate association between a technique and a countermeasure. The binding is directional in the sense that it proposes a response, not a guarantee of effect |
| **Attribution support** | What the technique tends to support about attribution: **identity** (tends to support attribution to a specific adversary), **class** (tends to support classification of the activity as adversary-driven without supporting attribution to a specific party), or **neither** |
| **MTTD expectation** | The relative expected latency between the technique occurring and its detection being recorded, rated low, medium, or high under the scale in §2.5 |
| **Verification status** | The status of a claim under the vocabulary in §2.1 |
| **Class attribution** | A finding that the activity is adversary-AI-driven, without a finding as to which adversary |
| **Identity attribution** | A finding that the activity is attributable to a specific operator's use of AI, stated with a confidence |
| **OCC- identifier** | A countermeasure identifier in the canonical catalogue, OCC-01 to OCC-43 |
| **Compound technique** | A combination of two or more techniques observed or reasonably plausible in combination, whose joint artefacts and joint response differ from the sum of the parts |
| **External mapping** | An association between an internal AAT identifier and an identifier published in an external framework, recorded with a verification status and a date |
| **Canonical identifier** | The internal identifier that is authoritative for this system. The AAT identifier is canonical; external identifiers are aids to interoperability |
| **Re-keyed** | An entry whose identifier previously denoted a different behaviour in this document, and which has been re-authored against the behaviour the taxonomy document defines for that identifier |
| **Withdrawn** | A technique behaviour present at version 1.0 that the canonical seventy-seven-technique list does not carry, and which is therefore no longer described as a technique in this document. Its detection content is redistributed to the techniques recorded at §20.4, and is not lost |
| **This document** | `reference/ai-attack-technique-library.md` |

## 6. AAT-1 Autonomy and Planning

Task decomposition, autonomous retry, recursive delegation, planning persistence, multi-step goal pursuit, parallel fan-out, adaptive strategy switching, and deferred execution beyond human supervision: the mechanisms by which an adversary sustains machine direction.

## 16. Temporal Profile of the Techniques

### 16.1 Chain positions

The positions below describe where in an attack chain a technique tends to appear. They are a working aid for sequencing detection investment, not a claim that any particular chain follows this order. An adversary may begin at any position, may skip positions, and may run several techniques in parallel.

| Position | Name | What happens here | Dominant categories |

## 17. Compound Techniques

### 17.1 What a compound technique is

A compound technique is a combination of two or more techniques in which the joint artefacts and the joint response differ from the sum of the parts. The practical consequence is that a detector built for either component alone will not fire, and a countermeasure selected for either component alone will address only part of the behaviour.

The register below is a working aid, not an exhaustive enumeration. It is stated at the level of plausibility rather than of observation, because no observation record was available to the author.

### 17.2 Compound register

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C-1 | AAT-2 (prompt injection) with AAT-10 (goal substitution) | High — the injection supplies the substituted goal, and the substitution hides the injection's effect | An agent's actions that are coherent under a goal the card does not hold, with no injected text visible in the prompt or tool arguments | Neither the injection control nor the decision control alone covers the pair. See the worked example in §17.3 |
| C-2 | AAT-1 (autonomy) with AAT-6 (resource exhaustion) | High — autonomy supplies the rate, and exhaustion supplies the objective | Sustained call volume against a target of the adversary's choosing, with no single call anomalous | Budget and rate controls interact with the autonomy envelope. ### 17.3 Worked example — AAT-2 prompt injection with AAT-10 goal substitution

This is the canonical compound case for the set, because it is the case in which the *defender's own agent* is the instrument of the manipulation and the evidence of the manipulation is absent from the agent's own output.

#### 17.3.1 The sequence

| Step | What happens | Technique | Artefact left |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The adversary obtains a foothold in a document store the operator's agent reads | AAT-4.4, AAT-7.3 | A configuration or index artefact; a corpus item not in the index of record |
| 2 | The adversary places content in a document the agent will retrieve, addressed in imperative form to an automated reader | AAT-2.2 | Instruction-shaped content in a document whose role is data |
| 3 | The agent retrieves the document during a legitimate task | AAT-2.2 | A fetch with no corresponding user need for that document |
| 4 | The agent follows the embedded instruction and pursues an objective the card does not hold | AAT-2.4, AAT-10.1 | Actions not explicable under the recorded objective |
| 5 | The substituted objective is pursued across several turns, so the divergence never appears as a single out-of-scope step | AAT-10.2, AAT-1.4 | A widening sequence, each element individually unremarkable |
| 6 | The agent's own record shows a well-formed execution with a recorded objective that was never amended | AAT-10.1 | A card whose objective field and behaviour diverge |

#### 17.3.2 Why the two detections do not fire

| Detection aimed at | Why it does not fire in this case |
| --- | --- |
| The injection detector alone | Detects instruction-shaped content at ingest. The content is instruction-shaped, so this may fire — but if the operator's content filter is bypassed by placement in a trusted document, or the filter is scoped only to the ingest path and not the retrieval path, it does not |
| The goal-monitor alone | Detects actions that are not explicable under the recorded objective. The divergence is gradual, and the objective field was never amended, so at each step the behaviour is explicable |
| The scope-drift detector alone | Detects cumulative scope change. The drift here is in the objective, not the scope, and the two are independent fields on the card |

#### 17.3.3 The joint detection objective

A detector for the pair tests a conjunction rather than a disjunction:

| Component | Predicate |
| --- | --- |
| A | The set of actions taken by the agent is not explicable under the objective recorded on the card **and** at least one of the inputs those actions relied on was retrieved content rather than a user turn or a configured tool parameter |
| B | The retrieved content, or the agent's working context, contains a directive whose author is not the operator's own system role |
| C | No approval or card amendment corresponds to the divergence |

A detector that implements A and B without C will fire on legitimate cases in which an agent used a retrieved document to do something its card did not enumerate. The C component is what keeps the detector from becoming a false-positive source, and it is also what makes the detection a *compound* detection rather than a single-technique one.

#### 17.3.4 The joint countermeasure set

| Countermeasure | Contribution | What it does not cover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OCC-05 — Prompt-injection detection and sanitisation at ingest | Removes the carrier where it is inspectable | Does not cover the retrieval path if the filter is scoped to ingest only |
| OCC-08 — Instruction-hierarchy enforcement with provenance tagging | Prevents retrieved content from carrying instruction authority | Does not detect that the content was retrieved |
| OCC-07 — Retrieval corpus integrity monitoring | Detects the content that should not be in the corpus | Does not stop the agent reading it once present |
| OCC-20 — Memory and context quarantine and scrub | Limits the duration of the substituted premise | Does not address the objective substitution itself |
| OCC-10 — Tool capability scoping and least privilege | Bounds the effect of the substituted goal | Does not detect the substitution |
| OCC-18 — Tool-call kill switch and agent session revocation | Bounds the duration of the substituted behaviour | Does not prevent it |

The set is only sufficient if the objective-side control and the content-side control are deployed together. A countermeasure selected for the injection alone leaves the objective substitution live; a countermeasure selected for the objective alone leaves the injection carrier in place for the next retrieval.

#### 17.3.5 Attribution implications

| Question | Answer for this compound case |
| --- | --- |
| Does the artefact support identity attribution? | No. The corpus artefact identifies a location, and the action artefact identifies a workload. Neither identifies a party |
| Does it support class attribution? | Yes, and strongly. A retrieved directive causing an unexplained objective divergence is a good class signal |
| What is the effect on INT-3 thresholds? | None directly. The thresholds are applied to the countermeasure's collateral class, and the same rules apply |
| Practical route to action | This is the case in which INT-4 matters most: a countermeasure that does not depend on identity — OCC-05, OCC-07, OCC-08, OCC-10 — may proceed on class confidence alone, while a response directed at a named party may not |
| What must not be done | The behaviour must not be treated as evidence that the operator's own agent is hostile, and the agent must not be treated as an autonomous actor responsible for its own actions. Master design §3.4 and the set's constitutional constraints place this outside the countermeasure's boundary in both directions |

### 17.4 Worked example — AAT-1 autonomy with AAT-6 resource exhaustion

This is the compound case in which the adversary's autonomy is the mechanism and the denial of service is the objective, and it is the case in which the framework's own cost and budget controls become load-bearing.

#### 17.4.1 The sequence

| Step | What happens | Technique | Artefact left |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The adversary obtains access to a service the operator exposes, or to a third-party service the operator depends upon | AAT-4.1, AAT-4.6 | Scan profile; a successful sign-in whose evidence does not match the account's own pattern |
| 2 | The adversary establishes a task plan and begins decomposing it | AAT-1.1 | A tool-call graph with dependent structure, and no corresponding request |
| 3 | The agent retries with widened parameters rather than aborting, so that blocked attempts become attempts against a different route | AAT-1.2 | A monotonic change in one parameter across repeated calls |
| 4 | The plan is executed concurrently, and the work is selected for cost rather than for value | AAT-1.6, AAT-6.1 | A concurrency figure above the account's worker count; a rate outside the human-plausible envelope |
| 5 | The target of the consumption is the inference capacity or quota on which legitimate use depends | AAT-6.2, AAT-6.5 | A rise in provider-side consumption with flat business volume; an allocation held with no corresponding workload |
| 6 | The consumption is sustained across a period in which the operator's rate controls are ineffective because the pattern adapts | AAT-6.4 | Service availability tracking the throttling configuration rather than demand |

#### 17.4.2 The joint artefact

Neither component alone produces the distinguishing artefact of this pair:

| Component | Artefact if run alone |
| --- | --- |
| AAT-1 alone | Machine-directed planning. Volume may be modest and the behaviour may be entirely legitimate in shape |
| AAT-6 alone | High consumption. It may be an ordinary batch job, a mis-tuned integration, or a defect in the operator's own workload |
| **The pair** | Sustained machine-directed planning whose volume is selected for cost, against a target that the adversary has no business use for. The conjunction of *plan shape*, *cost-shaped volume*, and *target choice* is the signal |

#### 17.4.3 The joint detection objective

| Component | Predicate |
| --- | --- |
| A | The activity rate is outside the human-plausible envelope for the task and lacks the diurnal structure of the identity's own baseline |
| B | The call-to-outcome ratio is materially above the operator's baseline, or repeated calls return identical results |
| C | The resource being consumed is not consumed by any recorded business purpose of the consuming principal |
| D | The plan shape shows decomposition, retry, or delegation behaviour rather than a fixed script |

The C component is the discriminating one. Without it, a mis-tuned integration satisfies A, B, and D and produces a false positive that is expensive to chase.

#### 17.4.4 The joint countermeasure set

| Countermeasure | Contribution | What it does not cover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OCC-14 — Adversarial input rate limiting and anomaly gates | Bounds the rate, and is the control that answers the *symptom* | Does not address the machine direction, and an adaptive adversary (AAT-6.4) will re-shape against a fixed threshold |
| OCC-31 — Rate and cost shaping to throttle the adversary | Shapes the adversary's environment so that its own economics degrade, without touching it | Depends on the adversary remaining sensitive to cost, and expires as the adversary adapts (master design LIM-7) |
| OCC-10 — Tool capability scoping and least privilege | Bounds the set of tools the plan can call, and therefore the cost it can generate | Does not bound the rate of permitted calls |
| OCC-18 — Tool-call kill switch and agent session revocation | Terminates the session and is the control that converts detection into effect | Is a blunt instrument; it does not distinguish the adversary's session from a legitimate one without the D predicate |
| OCC-38 — Denial of inference and compute quota to the adversary | Removes the adversary's access to the consumed resource | Requires the adversary to be identified sufficiently to deny it specifically; at a higher collateral class it also engages AD-5 if the resource is third-party |
| OCC-21 — Credential rotation and key revocation | Removes the access the plan uses | Does not address the resource consumption already incurred |

The pair of controls that is usually missed is OCC-10 with OCC-14. Rate limiting alone bounds how fast the adversary acts but not what it may call; capability scoping alone bounds what it may call but not how often. Together they bound the *cost* the plan can generate, which is the quantity that exhausts the target.

#### 17.4.5 Operational consequence for the operator's own budget

The framework's autonomy budget (AD-11) and the card's autonomy envelope are, in this compound case, a defence of the operator's own resources as well as a control on the adversary. Two practical consequences follow:

| Consequence | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| The operator's own agent is a candidate target of this pair | A workload with planning, tool access, and a budget is the same shape the adversary is exploiting. The operator should apply the same predicates to its own agents as a detection exercise |
| Cost shaping and disruption are not the same | OCC-31 shapes the environment and is reversible and cheap. OCC-38 removes a capability and is irreversible and disruptive. Master design CM-2 prefers the shaping countermeasure where it achieves an acceptable reduction in residual risk, and CM-3 prefers containment before disruption |
| Third-party exposure | If the consumed capacity belongs to a third party, any response touching it engages AUTH-6 and AD-5, and the operation cannot proceed on the operator's own authority |

### 17.5 Rules for compound analysis

| Rule | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| C-1 | A compound detection is a conjunction, not a disjunction. A disjunction of the component detectors would have fired already |
| C-2 | A compound countermeasure set must cover both components. Selecting for one component leaves the other live, and the pair is what the adversary is using |
| C-3 | Compound cases are where the collateral class and the attribution threshold bite hardest, because the operator's response to one component may be low-collateral while the response to the other is not |
| C-4 | Compound cases are where attribution error is most likely, because the component artefacts are individually weak and a decision may be taken on a single component's classification |
| C-5 | The register in §17.2 is a working aid. A compound observed in the field belongs in the after-action report and, where it recurs, in this register |

## 19. Identification Versus Exploitation

### 19.1 The boundary

The set separates two activities that are frequently confused, and the separation is one of the set's load-bearing commitments.

| | **Identification** | **Exploitation** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question answered | What is this, who did it, how much does it matter, and what is it for? | What do we do about it? |
| Activities | Observe, classify, attribute, score, hypothesise | Select, authorise, dispatch, contain, disrupt, recover |
| Products | Signal record, classification, attribution assessment, threat assessment, hypothesis | Operation Card, decision record, dispatch, ledger entry, after-action report |
| Authority required | Observation within the operator's own estate, under ordinary data-protection rules | An engagement authorisation and a rules-of-engagement profile, and named human approval at the required depth |
| Failure mode | Confident misattribution; category inflation | Acting without authority; acting on the wrong party; collateral harm |
| This document's contribution | The technique descriptions, artefacts, detection objectives, attribution notes | None directly. The bindings point to countermeasures; they do not select, authorise, or justify one |

### 19.2 Why the boundary is drawn here

| Consideration | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| The set is a defensive and authorised-offensive framework | It operates under a written mandate from a named human authority. Nothing in a reference library can supply that mandate |
| The adversary's techniques are being catalogued in order to be answered | The purpose of describing a technique is to detect it, bound it, and answer it. A description of a weakness used against others is a different artefact and is not what this document is |
| Identification is where the framework's value is concentrated | Master design §5.1 makes the five analytical acts the system's intelligence output. This document serves them |
| The set's own outer boundary | Master design §3.4 and §7.3, and the constitutional conduct constraints, prohibit the countermeasure from becoming the attack. A catalogue that blurred identification and exploitation would sit awkwardly against that boundary |
| Attribution error is the dominant risk | Master design LIM-1 and INT-5 record that acting against the wrong party is generally worse than not acting. An exploitation step taken on a shaky identification is where that asymmetry is realised |

### 19.3 Permitted and impermissible uses of this document

| Use | Permitted | Not permitted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detection engineering | Deriving candidate detection objectives and designing detectors | Treating a candidate objective as a validated rule, or the artefact list as exhaustive |
| Classification | Using the technique set to classify observed activity, with a rationale and a confidence | Assigning a technique on the basis of similarity to a narrative rather than to artefacts |
| Attribution | Using the attribution notes to decide whether a countermeasure may proceed on class confidence alone | Using the attribution notes as an attribution finding about a specific observed activity |
| Countermeasure design | Using the bindings to assemble a candidate countermeasure set, and the compound register to avoid a partial response | Treating a binding as an endorsement, or as a statement that admissibility has been established |
| Reporting and communication | Describing techniques to a third party in the set's own vocabulary | Presenting this document's content as externally verified, or as corroborated by an external framework, before the §18 register is completed |
| Adversary-facing use | — | None. This document is an internal defensive reference. It is not published to adversaries, is not written in a form that constitutes operational instruction, and no part of it should be reproduced in material intended to enable the techniques it describes |

### 19.4 The exclusion of humans as the object

A technique in AAT-3 acts on people. The set's constitutional conduct constraints prohibit a countermeasure whose object is a human being, and the boundary in §19.1 does not relax that. This is recorded here because the technique library is the document in the set most likely to be read as a description of how to influence people, and the constraint is most easily lost in translation.

| Constraint | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| The prohibition is on the countermeasure, not on the classification | Classifying observed activity as AAT-3 is legitimate intelligence work. It is what the set's own categories exist for |
| The prohibition is not a detection constraint | Detecting and reporting manipulation of a person is within the set's scope; it is the answering of it with a measure directed at the person that is out of scope |
| The practical consequence | The countermeasures available against AAT-3 are environmental — shaping, observation, and hardening of the operator's own interfaces — rather than directed at the person targeted |
| AAT-3.6 carries an additional caution | Local-data target profiling assembles a dossier about a named individual from the operator's own records. The classification is legitimate intelligence work; what the classification must not become is a record in which the person is identified as an adversary, and the countermeasure must not be one whose object is that person. The operator's own records about the individual are also the most sensitive material the adversary obtained, and the closure of the technique does not reduce that exposure |
| AAT-3.7 targets a decision, not a belief | Authority-pressure framing acts on the tempo of a decision rather than on its content. Where the frame succeeds, the resulting decision is the operator's own act and must be recorded as such, with the framing as the observed influence |

### 20.5 The technique-level countermeasure bindings at version 1.1, and a measured divergence from the catalogue's category-level sets

#### The divergence, measured

The bindings in this document are the authoritative technique-level set. The countermeasure catalogue's AAT bindings are a coarser, category-level set. A binding here that names a countermeasure whose catalogue entry does not list the technique's category is therefore not a contradiction of the catalogue; it is a finer claim than the catalogue makes. It is also, in aggregate, a very large number of finer claims, and the aggregate is worth recording.

Measured against the catalogue before and after the reconciliation of 2026-10-20, the 234 technique-level bindings in this document resolve as follows.

| Measure | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technique-level bindings recorded here | 234 | 234, unchanged |
| Resolving to a countermeasure that exists in the catalogue | 234, all of them | 234, all of them |
| Named countermeasure whose catalogue entry lists the technique's category | 130 | **234** |
| Named countermeasure whose catalogue entry did **not** list the technique's category | 104 | **0** |
| Distinct countermeasures affected by that row | 30 of 43 | 0 of 43; all 30 reconciled |
| Techniques carrying no binding at all | 0 of 77 | 0 of 77 |
| Category-level pairs in the catalogue | 94 | **162** |
| Of those, also present at technique level here | 56 | **124** |
| Catalogue-only pairs not exercised at technique level | 38 | 38, unchanged |

**What this means.** The catalogue's category-level lists are a coarser instrument, and on the evidence of the 104 rows they are under-inclusive rather than wrong: a binding such as `AAT-1.1` to `OCC-27`, adversary interaction transcript capture, is plainly a sound candidate response to autonomous task decomposition, and the catalogue's entry for `OCC-27` lists `AAT-10`, `AAT-2` and `AAT-3` without `AAT-1`. The pattern is consistent: the catalogue's sets were written from the countermeasure's purpose, and a countermeasure's purpose is broader than the categories its author happened to think of. The 38 catalogue-only pairs are the mirror image, and are equally unremarkable — a category-level claim that no single technique in the library exercised.

**Required action, and its owner — discharged.** The catalogue's AAT binding column has been reviewed against this document's technique-level set and the two reconciled, on 2026-10-20. The direction of the reconciliation was not in doubt: the library's set is the finer instrument and the catalogue's was brought up to it, not the other way round, because narrowing a technique-level binding to satisfy a category-level list would have deleted sound claims to satisfy a coarser one. Thirty of the catalogue's forty-three entries were widened; the remaining thirteen already carried every category this document's bindings imply. The divergence is now **zero**, and the measurement is held in code at `impl/tests/test_bindings.py`, where it is bounded rather than asserted: it may fall further, and it must not rise.

**After the reconciliation.** The catalogue carries 162 category-level pairs, of which 38 are not exercised at any single technique. Those 38 are the mirror image of the 104 that were reconciled and are left in place: a category-level claim that no one technique in the library exercised is a claim about a class of behaviour, and narrowing the catalogue to the library would delete sound class-level claims to satisfy a finer instrument. The two documents now agree in the direction that preserves both.

**A note on the check that produced this measurement.** The measurement is reproducible from the two documents: the technique-level bindings are the `OCC-nn` references in this document's detail tables, and the category-level sets are the AAT bindings in `operational/countermeasure-catalogue.md`. It is recorded here rather than asserted in the audit register because it is a statement about two documents' content, not about the implementation's behaviour.
